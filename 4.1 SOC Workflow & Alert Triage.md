## 🎯 Objective

Process security alerts using a structured triage methodology.

---

# 📋 Procedure

## 🚨 Step 1: Alert Queue Management

> **Purpose:** Ensure alerts are prioritised, assigned, and processed according to SOC procedures and Service Level Agreements (SLAs).

### Open the Alert Queue

Access your organization's SIEM/SOAR platform, such as:

* Splunk Enterprise Security (ES)
* Microsoft Sentinel
* ServiceNow SecOps

---

### Prioritise Alerts

Always process alerts in the following order:

1. 🔴 Critical
2. 🟠 High
3. 🟡 Medium
4. 🟢 Low

> ⚠️ **Never reverse this priority order.**

---

### Validate Before Investigating

Before beginning analysis, verify:

* Is this a scheduled false positive?
* Is this activity generated by a known-good internal tool?

---

### Assign Ownership

* Assign the alert to yourself before starting the investigation.
* Never leave alerts unassigned or without ownership.

---

### SLA Targets

| Severity    |    Response Time |
| ----------- | ---------------: |
| 🔴 Critical | **< 15 minutes** |
| 🟠 High     |     **< 1 hour** |
| 🟡 Medium   |    **< 4 hours** |
| 🟢 Low      |   **< 24 hours** |

---

## 🔍 Step 2: Triage an Alert (Structured Approach)

Follow this structured five-step methodology.

### ✅ Step 1 — Validate

Determine whether the alert is firing correctly.

Verify:

* Data source health
* Log availability
* Detection rule functionality

---

### 👤 Step 2 — Gather Context

Identify:

* User
* Host
* Asset criticality
* Business unit

---

### 🌐 Step 3 — Enrich

Query external threat intelligence sources, including:

* VirusTotal
* AlienVault OTX
* Talos Intelligence

---

### 🔗 Step 4 — Correlate

Determine whether related alerts exist for the same:

* User
* Host
* IP address

---

### ⚖️ Step 5 — Make a Decision

Choose the appropriate outcome:

* **True Positive** → Escalate
* **False Positive** → Document and tune the detection rule

---

## 📈 Step 3: Escalation Matrix

### 🟢 Level 1 (L1)

L1 analysts handle:

* Known alert patterns
* Standard playbooks
* Routine false positives

---

### 🟡 Escalate to Level 2 (L2)

Escalate when any of the following are identified:

* Unknown malware
* Confirmed compromise
* Lateral movement detected

---

### 🔴 Escalate to Level 3 (L3)

Escalate when investigating:

* Advanced Persistent Threat (APT) indicators
* Data exfiltration
* Critical asset compromise

---

### 🏢 Escalate to CISO / Management

Immediately escalate when incidents involve:

* Ransomware
* Breach notification triggers
* Regulatory impact

---

### Documentation Requirement

Always document:

* Escalation reason
* Timeline of actions

---

# ✅ Best Practice Tips

### 📌 Document Every Alert

Never close an alert without documenting:

* Decision made
* Evidence reviewed
* Next steps

---

### 📌 Context Matters

Context is critical during alert triage.

Example:

* A failed login from an IT administrator may be expected.
* A failed login against a service account at **3:00 AM** may require immediate investigation.

---

### 📌 Maintain Shift Handover Notes

Every analyst should know:

* What investigations were completed
* What remains outstanding
* Current incident status before the next shift begins

---

### 📌 Measure SOC Performance

Track and review weekly trends for:

* **MTTD** — Mean Time to Detect
* **MTTR** — Mean Time to Respond

---

### 📌 Rotate Analyst Responsibilities

Rotate analysts between different alert categories to:

* Prevent tunnel vision
* Improve cross-functional experience
* Build broader investigation skills
